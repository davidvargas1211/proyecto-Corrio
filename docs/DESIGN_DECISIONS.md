# Design decisions

## Narrative

The experience leads with the concrete task: paying suppliers in India from the UAE. It then earns trust by explaining an assessment rather than promising an instant transfer, confirmed quote or approval.

## Visual system

The visual language uses a restrained ink, paper and teal system. The central visual object is an assessment route, not a dashboard or transfer tracker. The goal is to make payment information feel calm, considered and readable.

## Responsive rules

Desktop introduces hierarchy through a two-column hero, editorial grids and a four-step process. Mobile uses an independent vertical composition: the payment conditions remain visible, quote pairs stack, navigation becomes a controlled menu and form actions expand to available width.

## Content rules

This is an illustrative experience. It avoids fees, exchange-rate numbers, payment time claims, regulatory claims, insurance claims, testimonials, customer logos, live support details and payment-status promises because they are not verified product facts in the challenge brief.
